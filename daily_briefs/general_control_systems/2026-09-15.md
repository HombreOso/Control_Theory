# Control Systems Brief — General Control Systems — 2026-09-15
_Backfilled brief — written retrospectively on 2026-09-21 for this date._

## Tech News
- MIT researchers built CW-Net, a system that translates a self-driving car's internal decisions into human-understandable concepts (e.g. "approaching stopped vehicle"), helping safety drivers anticipate vehicle behavior and intervene faster in testing. [System helps humans predict when self-driving cars will make mistakes | MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2026/system-helps-humans-predict-when-self-driving-cars-will-make-mistakes-0902)

## Research Papers
- Lin, Pasqualetti and Egerstedt show control barrier functions alone cannot quantify "how safe" a system is beyond a binary classification, and propose an "invariance authority demand" metric that is independent of the CBF representation chosen. [On the Degree of Safety: Beyond Safe or Unsafe with Control Barrier Functions (arXiv:2609.03319)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03319)
- A contact-aware graph neural network dynamics model paired with model-predictive path-integral control lets tensegrity (soft, compliant-strut) robots navigate obstacles, inclines and narrow passages more reliably than baseline MPC. [Model Predictive Control of Tensegrity Robots via Contact-Aware Graph Neural Dynamics Model (arXiv:2609.08958)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08958)
- A new "learning agent-based MPC" (LAMPC) uses Gaussian process regression to estimate the behavior of other agents in multi-vehicle traffic, with soft chance constraints for safety, and is shown to outperform traditional agent-based MPC. [Learning Agent-based Model Predictive Control for Holistic Vehicle Performance (arXiv:2609.11871)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11871)
- A design framework using frequency-domain tests and Lyapunov functions gives conditions under which control-barrier-function safety filters can be added to linear systems without destabilizing them. [Safe Stabilising Full-Order Affine Control Barrier Functions for Linear Systems (Extended) (arXiv:2609.12990)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12990)
- New work identifies a vulnerability in shared human-automation control where arbitration logic can be corrupted by unsafe automation, and proposes anchoring safety constraints to an unassisted pilot trajectory, demonstrated on F-16 pitch-tracking. [Barrier Functions Against Safety Drift in Shared Control (arXiv:2609.17701)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17701)
